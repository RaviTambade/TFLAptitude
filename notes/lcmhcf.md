## What is HCF?

The **Highest Common Factor (HCF)** is the **largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly without leaving any remainder**.

For example:

* Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
* Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

The largest common factor is **6**, so:

**HCF(12, 18) = 6**

HCF is useful whenever we need to **divide quantities into the largest possible equal groups**.


## What is LCM?

The **Least Common Multiple (LCM)** is the **smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more numbers**.

For example:

* Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, ...
* Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54, ...

The first common multiple is **36**, so:

**LCM(12, 18) = 36**

LCM is useful whenever we need to determine **when repeating events will occur together again**.

## A Simple Way to Remember

* **HCF answers:** *"What is the largest equal size?"*
* **LCM answers:** *"When or where will things come together again?"*

Understanding these two concepts not only helps solve mathematical problems but also builds the analytical thinking required in science, engineering, and software development.

## A simple rule to remember

* **Use HCF when you want to divide something into the largest equal parts without leftovers.**

  * Keywords: *largest equal size*, *maximum possible*, *divide evenly*, *no remainder*.

* **Use LCM when you want to find the first time or quantity where repeating events happen together.**

  * Keywords: *together again*, *simultaneously*, *next common occurrence*, *repeat cycle*.
